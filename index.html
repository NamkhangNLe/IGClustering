Findings

  • Everyone is 1 hop from Namkhang, 2 hops from everyone else. The data is 1-hop only, so the graph is a star: 790 nodes, 789 edges, diameter 2, average shortest path 1.9975, clustering coefficient 0. Any two people in this network are connected through exactly one intermediate, Namkhang.
  • 69.5% of the people Namkhang follows follow him back (397 of 571). 64.6% of his followers are people he follows back (397 of 615).
  • The non-mutuals split unevenly, and the following-only side is not what it looks like. 218 people follow Namkhang without a follow-back, versus 174 accounts he follows that don't follow him. Only 42 of those 174 (24%) match Georgia Tech / Barstool markers in their handle or name, and 101 of 174 (58%) carry an ordinary personal full name with no brand or meme marker at all. The data has no category labels, so any "mostly brands and meme pages" claim would be a guess, not a finding.
  • Mutuality, of following: 69.5%
  • Mutuality, of followers: 64.6%

What this does not answer

The original question, Ivy League vs Georgia Tech separation, cannot be answered from this dataset. The scrape collected username and full name only; there is no school affiliation anywhere in the data, and no second-hop edges (we never collected who anyone else follows). To answer it for real you would need, per person, a school label and at least one more hop of follow edges. Instagram no longer exposes that data through the endpoints this scraper used, so the honest finish is the ego-network analysis above, not an invented school clustering.

How it was done

MVP/scrape.js ran in the browser console against Instagram's old GraphQL endpoints and dumped followers / following as JSON (April 2024). MVP/merge.py joins the two lists into Merged.csv (the 397 mutuals). MVP/analyze.py validates the derived lists against the computed sets, computes the graph statistics, and writes MVP/results.json plus a static plot (ego_network.png). MVP/graphing.py re-renders that plot. This page is generated from the same JSON by make_index.py.
